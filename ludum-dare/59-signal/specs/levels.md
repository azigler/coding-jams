# CROSSED WIRES — Level content

All five authored levels, in the exact shape the impl will consume. Each
level's lines array is the on-screen order; the `owner` field is the ground
truth for win-checking.

The recurring name across the shift is **Ellen Whitaker**. She never
appears directly on a call; she's talked about. The story the player
untangles is the story of what happened to Ellen this week.

## Tone notes
- 1940s-ish small American town
- Formal-but-warm epistolary register
- Conversations are clipped — phone calls, not letters
- Each line ≤ 70 characters for clean monospace layout

## Level 1 — "Hello?"

**Epigraph:** *"Every shift begins with the same crossed line."*

**Conversations:**
- A: **the mother** (warm amber)
- B: **the office** (ink-blue)

**Lines** (authored order, hidden owner):

| # | Line | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hello? Are you there? | A |
| 2 | The meeting is at three, not four. | B |
| 3 | I was hoping to catch you before you went out. | A |
| 4 | Did you bring the contracts? | B |
| 5 | The forecast says rain — remember the umbrella. | A |
| 6 | Put them on my desk. I'll sign after lunch. | B |
| 7 | I love you, dear. | A |
| 8 | Everyone in the room needs to hear this. | B |

**Postamble (on solve):**
*The mother hangs up first. The office talks to itself for another twenty
minutes. The operator listens and doesn't say a word.*

## Level 2 — "Wednesday"

**Epigraph:** *"Some calls sound the same. They aren't."*

Difficulty tick: both conversations are business voices, distinguishing
features live in topic + names.

**Conversations:**
- A: **the insurance office** (amber) — about a claim
- B: **the law office** (blue) — about a will

**Lines:**

| # | Line | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The adjuster's report came through this morning. | A |
| 2 | Mrs. Whitaker wants the signing moved to Friday. | B |
| 3 | The damage exceeds the policy's limit by four hundred. | A |
| 4 | She's been widowed six months. Be gentle. | B |
| 5 | Can we split the payout across two quarters? | A |
| 6 | The estate is small — mostly the house on Maple. | B |
| 7 | I'll need the signature by end of day Thursday. | A |
| 8 | Her sister is contesting the second page. | B |
| 9 | Tell the client we'll cover half in good faith. | A |
| 10 | Friday at ten. Don't let her bring the sister. | B |

**Postamble:**
*Two voices, two desks, one name between them. The operator makes a small
note and sets it aside.*

## Level 3 — "The Shift"

**Epigraph:** *"Three lines, all ringing at once. All of them matter."*

Difficulty tick: three-way crossed line. Ten lines, three conversations of
varying length.

**Conversations:**
- A: **the doctor's office** (amber) — a test result
- B: **the school** (blue) — a parent-teacher concern
- C: **the neighbor** (sage) — a noise complaint

**Lines:**

| # | Line | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The results came back this morning. I'm afraid — | A |
| 2 | He's been distracted in class all week. | B |
| 3 | — it's not what we hoped. Can you come in tomorrow? | A |
| 4 | There's been shouting, late. After ten. | C |
| 5 | I'd rather discuss it in person. Both of you, if possible. | B |
| 6 | The Whitaker place. I've called them twice. | C |
| 7 | Ten in the morning. Don't bring him. | A |
| 8 | He's a bright child. We don't want to label anything. | B |
| 9 | I don't want trouble. I just want to sleep. | C |
| 10 | I know. I know. I'll tell her tonight. | A |

**Postamble:**
*The doctor, the teacher, and the neighbor. Three different rooms in the
same town — and the operator hears them layered like a chord.*

## Level 4 — "Dearest"

**Epigraph:** *"One voice knows it's being overheard."*

Difficulty tick: two conversations, but the second caller is DELIBERATELY
speaking in coded language to disguise the real topic. The "disguise"
conversation sounds like something innocent (a garden chat) but is really
about something else (an affair, a secret plan). Works as a puzzle because
the disguise is thin once you look — the A-caller is having a normal
conversation, the B-caller is saying "the geraniums are blooming by the
shed" when they mean "Ellen has agreed to meet."

**Conversations:**
- A: **the grocer** (amber) — a delivery order, prosaic
- B: **the letter** (blue) — a coded lover's message

**Lines:**

| # | Line | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two pounds of flour and a tin of coffee, yes. | A |
| 2 | The geraniums are blooming by the shed. | B |
| 3 | Can you send it around after four? | A |
| 4 | She said she'd come once the ice breaks. | B |
| 5 | The bill with the others — Mrs. Harrow pays monthly. | A |
| 6 | I'll leave the kitchen door unlatched. | B |
| 7 | And a pound of sugar if there's any left. | A |
| 8 | The husband travels Thursdays. He's always gone by eight. | B |
| 9 | Thank you. Tell your wife hello. | A |
| 10 | Burn this when you're done. | B |

**Postamble:**
*The grocer orders two pounds of flour. The letter burns in a different
fireplace. Ellen Whitaker's kitchen door will not be locked tonight.*

## Level 5 — "Dial Tone"

**Epigraph:** *"The last call of the shift. Nobody is speaking."*

Difficulty tick: the lines are fragments of an incoming emergency call. Two
voices — the caller and the dispatcher — but the caller is panicking and
the dispatcher is calm. The player has heard Ellen's name threaded through
the shift. Now she's the one calling.

**Conversations:**
- A: **the caller** (amber) — Ellen
- B: **the dispatcher** (blue) — calm, procedural

**Lines:**

| # | Line | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Please — please, there's been — | A |
| 2 | Ma'am, I need you to take a breath and tell me the address. | B |
| 3 | Forty-two Maple. The kitchen. He — | A |
| 4 | Stay on the line. An ambulance is leaving the station now. | B |
| 5 | I thought he had gone. I thought it was Thursday. | A |
| 6 | You're doing fine. Keep talking to me. | B |
| 7 | There's so much blood. It isn't mine. It isn't mine. | A |
| 8 | Help is three minutes away. Can you tell me your name? | B |
| 9 | Ellen. Ellen Whitaker. | A |
| 10 | Ellen, I'm Ruth. I'm not going anywhere. Stay with me. | B |

**Postamble:**
*The operator is Ruth. The operator has been Ruth all along. This is the
shift where Ellen Whitaker's name stops being just a name on the board.*

*Every shift has a last call. Some of them pass through you and leave no
mark. This one will not.*

**End-of-game card:**
*Thank you for taking the shift.*
