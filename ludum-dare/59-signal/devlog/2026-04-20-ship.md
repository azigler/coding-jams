Made a phone puzzle over the weekend. Rewrote it four times first.

**What it is:** you're a 1940s switchboard operator. Two unrelated callers ended up tangled on the same wire. Sort each line back to where it belongs.

## What I threw out

- **Phase Chorus.** Fireflies sync their pulses via Kuramoto coupling. Beautiful physics. Almost no agency — the system converges on its own. Prototyped it, played it, admitted it.
- **Steganogram.** Hide a secret word inside a cover letter by swapping synonyms. Couldn't judge "does the cover still read naturally" without an LLM. Shelved.
- **Switchboard, but a murder mystery.** Same frame, small-town narrative threaded through five shifts. Too much writing for 72 hours solo.

What survived was the crossed-line kernel. Two calls. One wire. You sort.

## The unlock

First pass alternated A/B/A/B and read like clerical work. Second pass: merge the transcript by *simulated timestamp* (one caller monologues while the other is silent, just like a real crossed line), then let **static eat the topic words** on some lines.

What's left is the caller's *voice*. Mrs. Kellerman gushes in ALL-CAPS. Larry answers in two words. Roz the pet psychic speaks in past tense about your dead canary. Once you've heard a caller for a line or two, you can place the static-eaten ones by cadence alone.

## The finale

A wedding cake order crossed with a hamster funeral. Closed casket. Egg salad. Fondant. Cheddar. Nugget the hamster was, apparently, *everything*.
